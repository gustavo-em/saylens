# Name analysis

## The frame I should have used first

Trademark strength runs on a ladder, and where a name sits on it decides how
protectable it is:

1. **Fanciful** — invented words (Kodak, Xerox, Spotify). Strongest.
2. **Arbitrary** — real words with no relation to the product (Apple for
   computers). Strong.
3. **Suggestive** — hints at a quality without describing it (Netflix). Medium.
4. **Descriptive** — states what the product does. Weak, and often refused
   registration without proof of acquired distinctiveness.
5. **Generic** — unregistrable.

**Aloud** is descriptive: it names the behaviour the app is built around. It is a
common English word, and the more common the word and the closer it sits to the
function, the less of it you can own. Good instinct to stop.

**SayLens has the same defect.** Say + Lens describes the mechanism exactly, which
is why it is easy to understand and hard to protect — and it sits next to Google
Lens in the one interaction they share. Keeping it is not the safe default it
looks like; it is the same weakness plus an adjacency problem.

**SpellForMe** stays dropped: wrong verb (spelling is the one thing the app does
not teach) and a passive promise that undercuts a learning product.

## Shortlist

The target is fanciful or arbitrary, two or three syllables, pronounceable by a
Brazilian and an English speaker.

| Candidate | Type | Case for | Case against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nomo** | fanciful | From *nome* / *nomen*. Invented, so it starts high on the ladder. Two syllables, no pronunciation friction in either language, clean domain shape. Gestures at naming without describing the mechanism. | Short invented names are heavily squatted; the domain will likely cost money. |
| **Vocca** | fanciful | Built from a voice root and doubled to make it invented. Reads premium, works in PT and EN unchanged. | The voice root makes it lean suggestive rather than fully fanciful. |
| **Sabiá** | arbitrary | The Brazilian thrush — a bird known for its song, and a bird name says nothing about language learning, so it is arbitrary and strong. Culturally rooted at home. | The accent and the *-iá* ending are real friction for English speakers, and the ASCII form collides with Spanish and Portuguese *sabia*. |
| **Farol** | arbitrary | Lighthouse. Optical without being literal about cameras, easy in PT and ES, manageable in EN. | Closer to suggestive than the others, and it is a common word in Portuguese. |

One more worth considering for a Brazil-first launch: **Bem-te-vi**, the bird
named after the phrase it appears to say — and the phrase means "I saw you well."
See and say are both in the name. It is the most conceptually accurate option in
this document and the least exportable; the hyphens and three syllables do not
travel.

## What I cannot do

None of the above is clearance. Before committing to any name, someone has to
check INPI (Brazil), USPTO or EUIPO if the app will be sold abroad, App Store and
Play Store listings, and domain availability. That search is the deciding input,
not the shortlist.

## Recommendation

Do the clearance search on two or three of the shortlist before any code changes.
A rename costs the bundle identifier, the store listing, the domain and every
asset, so it should happen once, on a name that survived a search — not on the
first name that sounded right.
